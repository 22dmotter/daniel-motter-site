Why it matters

Why debt hurts more than you think.

Debt doesn't just cost what you borrowed. Interest makes everything cost more, every payment spends a paycheck you haven't earned yet, and when payments eat your budget there's no room for surprises, so the next emergency becomes more debt.

It also costs things that don't show up on a statement: stress you carry home, choices you can't make, and money you can't give. Run your numbers in the calculator above and you'll see it clearly.

The plan

7 steps to get out of debt.

Simple doesn't mean easy, but these steps work if you stick with them.

Step 1

List every debt.

Write down each debt with its balance, interest rate, and minimum payment. Seeing the full number is uncomfortable, but you can't beat what you won't look at.

Step 2

Stop borrowing.

Pause new debt while you pay off the old. Put the cards away, delete saved cards from shopping apps, and decide ahead of time that new debt isn't an option.

Step 3

Build a small starter emergency fund.

Set aside a small cushion, even a few hundred dollars, so a surprise expense doesn't go straight back on a credit card.

Step 4

Make a simple budget.

Give every dollar a job before the month starts. Cover needs first, then minimum payments, then send everything extra to debt.

Step 5

Pick your payoff method.

Choose snowball (smallest balance first) or avalanche (highest interest first). Either works if you stick with it. See the comparison below.

Step 6

Attack one debt at a time.

Pay minimums on everything, and throw every extra dollar at your target debt. When it's gone, roll that whole payment into the next one.

Step 7

Track it and celebrate.

Watch the total drop month by month. Celebrate every debt you knock out, cheaply. Momentum keeps you going.

Speed it up

6 ways to find extra money for debt.

Every extra dollar shortens your payoff date. Plug any amount into the calculator's "extra" box to see the difference.

  • Sell things you don't use anymore.
  • Pick up extra shifts or a side gig and send all of it to debt.
  • Cancel subscriptions you forgot you had.
  • Cook at home more and plan meals for the week.
  • Call your card company and ask for a lower interest rate.
  • Put any tax refund, bonus, or gift money straight toward debt.

FAQ

Common questions about getting out of debt.

Why is debt bad?

Debt commits your future income to paying back the past. Interest makes everything cost more, monthly payments shrink your margin, and owing money limits your freedom to save, give, and make choices.

Should I use the debt snowball or the avalanche?

The avalanche (highest interest first) usually costs less in interest. The snowball (smallest balance first) gives quicker wins that keep many people motivated. The best method is the one you'll actually stick with.

Should I save or pay off debt first?

Many people keep a small starter emergency fund first so surprises don't create new debt, then put everything extra toward debt.

What does the Bible say about debt?

Romans 13:8 says, "Let no debt remain outstanding, except the continuing debt to love one another." Scripture points us toward getting free of debt so we can live and give generously.

How long will it take to get out of debt?

It depends on your balances, interest rates, and how much you can pay each month. Use the calculator on this page to estimate your own payoff date.
