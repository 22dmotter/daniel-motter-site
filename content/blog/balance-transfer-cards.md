## How a balance transfer works

1. You apply for a card with a **promotional balance transfer APR**, often 0% for a set period.
2. If approved, you ask the new issuer to pay off your old card balances, up to your new credit limit.
3. The new card charges a **balance transfer fee**, typically added to your balance.
4. During the promotion, more of each payment goes to principal because little or no interest is charged.
5. When the promo ends, any remaining balance starts accruing interest at the card's regular APR.

Transfers between cards from the **same bank** usually aren't allowed.

## A worked example

You owe **$6,000 at 24% APR**. You qualify for a **0% transfer for 15 months** with a **3% fee**.

- Transfer fee: $6,000 × 3% = **$180**, so the new balance is **$6,180**
- Payment needed to clear it in 15 months: $6,180 ÷ 15 = **$412 a month**

| Option | Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Interest and fees |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stay at 24% APR | $412 | About 18 months | About $1,164 interest |
| 0% transfer, 3% fee | $412 | 15 months | $180 fee |

**Savings:** about **$984** and **three months**, if you pay it off within the promo period and make no new charges.

## The fine print to check before applying

- **Promo length:** how many months at the intro rate
- **Transfer fee:** often a percentage of the transferred amount, sometimes with a minimum dollar amount
- **Transfer deadline:** many offers only apply to transfers made within a certain window after opening
- **Regular APR after the promo:** what you'll pay on any remaining balance
- **Purchase APR:** new purchases may not get the 0% rate. Ideally, don't use the card for purchases at all
- **Late payment rules:** a late payment can end your promotional rate early on some cards
- **Credit limit:** you may not get a high enough limit to move everything

## Who balance transfers work best for

- People with **good to excellent credit** who can qualify for long promos
- Those with a **realistic plan** to pay the balance off within the promo period
- People who have **stopped adding new debt**

## Risks and how to avoid them

### Running out of time

**Divide the transferred balance plus fee by the number of promo months** to get your required payment. Automate it. If you can't afford that amount, a balance transfer may still help, but plan for the remaining balance and its future APR.

### Running the old cards back up

With the old cards at zero, it's tempting to use them again. That can double your debt. Remove them from your wallet and online accounts.

### Deferred interest confusion

A true 0% intro APR doesn't charge back interest when the promo ends. Some **store card** promotions use **deferred interest**, where back interest can be charged if the balance isn't paid in full. Read the terms.

### Credit score effects

Applying causes a hard inquiry and a new account, which can lower your score slightly for a while. Over time, lower balances and on-time payments usually help. See [how credit scores work](how-credit-scores-work.html).

| Option | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0% balance transfer | Good credit, payoff possible within promo | Fees, promo end, new spending |
| [Personal loan](personal-loan-to-pay-off-credit-cards.html) | Fixed payment and end date | Origination fees, rate depends on credit |

## What if you don't qualify?

If you're declined or approved for a limit too small to help, don't keep applying. Each application can add a hard inquiry. Instead:

- Transfer what you can and attack the rest with the [avalanche method](debt-avalanche-method.html).
- Call your current issuers to [ask for a lower APR](negotiate-lower-interest-rate.html).
- Work on the factors that improve approval odds: on-time payments and lower utilization. Then try again in six to twelve months.
- Ask a nonprofit credit counselor about a [debt management plan](credit-counseling-debt-management-plan.html).

## Doing the math on partial payoff

Suppose you can only pay $300 a month on the $6,180 transferred balance. After 15 months you'd have paid $4,500, leaving $1,680 that starts accruing interest at the regular APR. That's still far better than paying 24% on the full $6,000 the whole time, but plan ahead. Keep paying the same amount after the promo ends, or look at a second option for the remainder before the deadline.

## Tips for success

- Make the transfer **as soon as you're approved** to stay within any transfer window.
- **Keep paying the old card** until you confirm the transfer posted.
- Set autopay for the amount needed to finish before the promo ends, not just the minimum.
- **Mark the promo end date** on your calendar a few months early.
- Put the transfer card's payment at the top of your budget.

@faq
Q: Does a balance transfer hurt your credit?
A: Applying adds a hard inquiry and a new account, which can lower your score slightly at first. Paying down the balance and lowering utilization often improves your score over time.
Q: Is a balance transfer fee worth it?
A: It is if the interest you'd save is bigger than the fee. On high-APR debt you can pay off within the promotion, the savings are usually far larger than a few percent.
Q: Can I transfer a balance to a card from the same bank?
A: Usually not. Most issuers don't allow transfers between their own cards.
Q: What happens when the 0% period ends?
A: Any remaining balance starts accruing interest at the card's regular APR. Some store cards use deferred interest instead, which may charge interest back to the purchase date.
