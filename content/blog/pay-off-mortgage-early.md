## The math: what extra payments do

**Example:** $250,000 mortgage at **6.5%** for **30 years**. The principal and interest payment is about **$1,580.17**, and total interest over 30 years is about **$318,862**.

| Strategy | Payoff time | Total interest | Interest saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regular payments | 30 years | About $318,862 | None |
| One extra payment a year (13/12 of the payment monthly) | About 24 years, 2 months | About $246,148 | About $72,700 |
| Extra $200 a month | About 22 years, 1 month | About $221,243 | About $97,600 |
| Extra $500 a month | About 16 years, 3 months | About $155,345 | About $163,500 |

These figures cover principal and interest only. Taxes, insurance and any mortgage insurance are separate. Run your own numbers with our [debt payoff calculator](../calculate-your-payment.html).

## Why extra payments are so powerful early on

Early in a mortgage, most of each payment goes to **interest**. In the example, the first month's interest is about $1,354, so only about $226 goes to principal. An extra $200 nearly doubles the principal reduction that month, and that principal never accrues interest again. See [extra payments on loans](extra-payments-on-loans.html).

## What to do before paying extra

A mortgage is often one of the lowest-rate debts you have. Before sending extra money to it, consider this order:

1. **Starter emergency fund**, then a fuller one of three to six months. See [how much emergency fund](how-much-emergency-fund.html).
2. **Employer retirement match**, often worth getting first
3. **High-interest debt** like credit cards and personal loans. See [debt avalanche](debt-avalanche-method.html).
4. **Retirement savings** toward your goal
5. **Other goals**, such as kids' education or a car fund
6. **Extra mortgage payments**

Many people work on several of these at once. The point is not to pay down a 6.5% mortgage while carrying a 24% credit card.

## Pay off the mortgage or invest?

It's a classic question. There's no single right answer, but here's how to think about it:

| Pay down the mortgage | Invest the extra |
| --- | --- |
| Guaranteed return equal to your rate | Potentially higher long-term returns, but not guaranteed |
| Lower risk and lower monthly expenses later | Money stays accessible, depending on the account |
| Peace of mind and a paid-off home | Tax-advantaged accounts can add benefits |
| Money is locked in the house | Market drops can hurt in the short term |

The **higher your mortgage rate**, the stronger the case for paying it down. The **lower your rate**, the stronger the case for investing, especially in tax-advantaged retirement accounts. Many people split extra money between the two. See [pay off debt or invest](pay-off-debt-or-invest.html).

## Ways to pay off a mortgage early

### Add a fixed amount each month

Even $50 or $100 a month helps. Tell your servicer to apply the extra to **principal**.

### Make one extra payment a year

Divide your monthly payment by 12 and add that amount each month, or make one extra full payment annually. In the example, that saves about $72,700.

### Biweekly payments

Paying half your payment every two weeks results in 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments a year. **Check with your servicer first.** Some hold partial payments until they have a full payment, and some third-party biweekly programs charge fees. You can get the same effect free by adding 1/12 of a payment each month.

### Recast after a lump sum

Some lenders allow a **recast**: you make a large principal payment and the lender recalculates a lower monthly payment over the same remaining term, usually for a fee. That lowers your required payment, but if your goal is to finish early, keep paying the old amount.

### Refinance to a shorter term

Refinancing from 30 years to 15 years usually comes with a lower rate and much less interest, but a higher monthly payment and closing costs. Only refinance if the payment is comfortably affordable.

## Check for prepayment penalties

Most modern home loans don't have prepayment penalties, but some do. Review your loan documents or ask your servicer before making large extra payments.

## The non-math benefits

A paid-off home means **much lower required monthly expenses**, which makes job changes, retirement and emergencies easier to handle. Many people value that security more than a possible higher return elsewhere, and that's a legitimate choice.

## The downside: money is less accessible

Every extra dollar in your home is harder to get back. To access it, you'd need to sell, refinance or borrow against your equity. That's why an emergency fund comes first, and why it's important not to put every spare dollar into the mortgage if your savings are thin.

Q: Is it smart to pay off your mortgage early?
A: It can be, once you have an emergency fund, no high-interest debt and solid retirement savings. It saves interest and lowers expenses, but the money becomes less accessible.
Q: How much does an extra $200 a month save on a mortgage?
A: On a $250,000 mortgage at 6.5% for 30 years, an extra $200 a month cuts payoff to about 22 years and saves about $97,600 in interest. Your results depend on your balance and rate.
Q: Are biweekly mortgage payments worth it?
A: They result in one extra payment a year, which shortens the loan. Check how your servicer handles them and avoid programs with fees. Adding 1/12 of a payment each month does the same thing.
Q: Should I pay off my mortgage or invest?
A: It depends on your rate, risk tolerance and goals. Higher mortgage rates favor paying down the loan; lower rates favor investing. Many people do some of each.
