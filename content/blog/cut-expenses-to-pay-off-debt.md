## Start with a spending audit

Before cutting, look at what you actually spend. Pull 60 to 90 days of bank and card statements and sort every charge into categories. Highlight:

- **Recurring charges** you'd forgotten about
- **Categories much higher than you expected**, often food
- **Fees**: overdraft, late, ATM, foreign transaction

This step alone often finds money. Many banking apps can sort transactions into categories automatically, which makes the audit faster.

## Why big cuts beat small cuts

A $5 daily coffee adds up, but one decision on a big bill can match months of small sacrifices.

| Change | Monthly savings | Yearly savings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lowering rent by $200 with a roommate or move | $200 | $2,400 |
| Shopping car insurance and saving $40 | $40 | $480 |
| Canceling three $15 subscriptions | $45 | $540 |
| Switching to a cheaper phone plan, saving $35 | $35 | $420 |
| Cooking at home four more nights a month, saving $15 a meal | $60 | $720 |
| **Total** | **$380** | **$4,560** |

These are illustrations. Your savings depend on your costs.

## What $300 a month can do

**Example:** $10,000 of credit card debt at **22% APR**. Paying **$300 a month**, it takes about **52 months** and costs about **$5,596** of interest. Raising the payment to **$600 a month** cuts that to about **21 months** and about **$2,043** of interest, saving roughly **$3,550**.

Use the [debt payoff calculator](../calculate-your-payment.html) to see your own numbers.

## Big fixed costs

### Housing

1. **Get a roommate** or rent a room
2. **Move somewhere cheaper** at the end of your lease
3. **Negotiate rent** at renewal, especially if you've been a reliable tenant
4. **Refinance a mortgage** if rates and closing costs make sense

### Transportation

5. **Sell a car you can't afford** and buy a cheaper, reliable one
6. **Go down to one car** if your household can manage
7. **Refinance a high-rate auto loan.** See [pay off car loan early](pay-off-car-loan-early.html).
8. **Use transit, biking or carpooling** where practical

### Insurance

9. **Shop auto and home or renters insurance** every year or two
10. **Raise deductibles** once your emergency fund can cover them
11. **Bundle policies** if it lowers total cost

### Phone, internet and subscriptions

12. **Switch to a lower-cost phone plan**
13. **Call your internet provider** and ask about current promotions or cheaper plans
14. **Cancel unused subscriptions**, and rotate streaming services instead of keeping them all
15. **Check app store subscriptions**, which are easy to forget

## Everyday spending

### Food

16. **Plan meals** for the week and shop with a list
17. **Cook in batches** and pack lunches
18. **Cut back on delivery**, which often includes fees and higher menu prices
19. **Buy store brands** for staples

### Shopping

20. **Use a 48-hour rule** for nonessential purchases
21. **Unsubscribe from store emails** and remove saved cards from shopping sites
22. **Buy used** for furniture, tools and kids' gear

### Fees and interest

23. **Ask for late fees to be waived** and set up autopay to avoid more
24. **Opt out of overdraft coverage** or set balance alerts
25. **Call card issuers for a lower rate.** See [negotiate a lower interest rate](negotiate-lower-interest-rate.html).

## Make the savings count

Cutting expenses only speeds up debt payoff if the money actually goes to debt. The best approach:

1. **Decide the amount** you're freeing up, such as $150 from a phone and insurance change
2. **Increase your automatic debt payment** by that amount right away
3. **Send one-time savings**, like a refund or sold item, to debt the same day
4. **Track it**, so you can see the impact

This is how small changes turn into real progress with the [debt snowball](debt-snowball-method.html) or [avalanche](debt-avalanche-method.html).

## Don't cut so deep you quit

Extreme budgets often fail. Keep:

- **A small amount of fun money**
- **Essentials for health and work**
- **Some giving**, if that's important to you
- **A starter emergency fund**, so surprises don't create new debt

A plan you can follow for two years beats a perfect plan you abandon in two months.

## Cutting expenses with a partner or family

Talk about goals before cuts. Agree on what matters most, pick shared changes, and let each person keep a little personal money. Celebrate milestones together, like each debt paid off.

## Pair cutting with earning

There's a limit to how much you can cut, but there's more room to grow income. Combining both is the fastest route. See [side income to pay off debt](side-income-to-pay-off-debt.html).

Q: What expenses should I cut first to pay off debt?
A: Start with big fixed costs like housing, transportation, insurance and subscriptions, since one change saves money every month. Then trim food and shopping.

A: Usually not. A small amount of fun money makes a budget easier to stick with over the long run.
