## How payday loans work

1. You borrow a small amount, often a few hundred dollars.
2. You give the lender a post-dated check or access to your bank account.
3. The full amount plus a fee is due in a lump sum, usually in about two weeks.
4. If you can't repay, many lenders let you **roll over** or renew the loan by paying another fee.

Rules vary a lot by state. Some states ban payday loans or cap their costs, and others allow them with fewer limits.

## The real cost: nearly 400% APR

The [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/) explains that a typical two-week payday loan with a **$15 per $100** fee equals an APR of almost **400%**.

**The math:** $15 ÷ $100 = 15% for 14 days. There are about 26 two-week periods in a year, so 15% × (365 ÷ 14) ≈ **391% APR**.

Compare that with credit cards, which commonly range from the high teens to the high twenties, and personal loans, which are usually much lower. See [APR vs. interest rate](apr-vs-interest-rate.html).

## The rollover trap

**Example:** You borrow **$400** with a **$60** fee due in two weeks. Payday comes, and you can't spare $460. You pay $60 to roll it over.

| Two-week period | Fee paid | Still owe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 (original loan) | $60 | $400 |
| 2 (first rollover) | $60 | $400 |
| 3 (second rollover) | $60 | $400 |
| 4 (third rollover) | $60 | $400 |
| 5 (fourth rollover) | $60 | $400 |

After about ten weeks, you've paid **$300 in fees** and **still owe the full $400**. That's why payday loans can become a cycle that's hard to escape.

## Other risks

- **Bank fees:** repeated withdrawal attempts on an account with too little money can trigger overdraft or returned-payment fees from your bank
- **Collections** if you default
- **Access to your bank account** can make it hard to prioritize essentials like rent and food

## Car title loans are risky too

**Title loans** use your car as collateral, often with very high costs. If you can't repay, the lender can **repossess your car**, which can make it harder to get to work. Treat them with the same caution as payday loans.

## Better alternatives

### Credit union payday alternative loans (PALs)

Many federal credit unions offer **payday alternative loans** with interest capped at **28% APR**, much lower than payday loans, and repayment over months instead of two weeks. You may need to be a member.

### Payment plans with whoever you owe

Call the utility, landlord, hospital or lender and ask for **more time or a payment plan**. Many have hardship programs. This often costs little or nothing.

### Paycheck advance from your employer

Some employers offer pay advances, sometimes free. Understand any fees and how repayment works.

### Local and community assistance

Nonprofits, charities, religious organizations and government programs may help with rent, utilities, food or emergencies. Calling **211** or visiting **211.org** can connect you to local resources.

### A small personal loan

If you have fair credit, a small personal loan from a bank or credit union usually costs far less.

### Sell something or pick up extra work

One-time income can cover a short gap. See [side income to pay off debt](side-income-to-pay-off-debt.html).

### Negotiate with creditors

If you're behind on several bills, a **nonprofit credit counselor** can help you plan. See [credit counseling and debt management plans](credit-counseling-debt-management-plan.html).

## Questions to ask before any short-term loan

- What is the APR, including all fees?
- What is the total amount I'll repay, in dollars?
- When is each payment due, and can I afford it while still paying rent and buying food?
- What happens if I'm late or can't pay?
- Does the lender need access to my bank account?

If the lender can't give clear answers, walk away.

## Protections for military members

The federal **Military Lending Act** generally caps the cost of many consumer loans, including payday loans, at **36% Military APR** for active-duty service members and their dependents.

## If you already have a payday loan

1. **Stop rolling over** if possible. Ask whether your state requires the lender to offer an **extended payment plan**.
2. **Pay it off with a cheaper option**, such as a credit union PAL.
3. **Know your rights.** You may be able to revoke the lender's authorization to debit your account. Check with your bank and the CFPB.
4. **Report problems** to the CFPB or your state regulator.

## Break the cycle for good

The long-term fix is a buffer. Even a **$500 starter emergency fund** can replace the need for many payday loans. See [how much emergency fund](how-much-emergency-fund.html) and [how to stop living paycheck to paycheck](stop-living-paycheck-to-paycheck.html).

Q: Why are payday loans bad?
A: They have very high costs. A typical $15 per $100 fee for two weeks equals nearly 400% APR, and many borrowers roll loans over repeatedly, paying fees without reducing what they owe.
Q: What is a payday alternative loan?
A: A small-dollar loan offered by many federal credit unions with APR capped at 28% and longer repayment terms than payday loans.
Q: Can a payday lender take money from my bank account?
A: If you authorized it, they can attempt withdrawals. Repeated failed attempts can cause bank fees. You may be able to revoke authorization; check with your bank and the CFPB.
Q: What can I do instead of a payday loan?
A: Ask for a payment plan, look into credit union alternative loans or employer advances, contact local assistance through 211, or sell something or pick up extra work.
