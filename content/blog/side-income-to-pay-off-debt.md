## Why income matters as much as cutting

There's a floor to how much you can cut, but there's more room to grow income. Even a modest increase, sent entirely to debt, can change your timeline dramatically.

**Example:** $15,000 of debt at **20% APR**:

| Monthly payment | Payoff time | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $400 | About 60 months | About $8,736 |
| $800 (+$400 side income) | About 23 months | About $3,136 |

An extra $400 a month cuts payoff by about three years and saves about **$5,600**. See it with your own numbers in our [calculator](../calculate-your-payment.html).

## Start with your main job

Your primary job is often the biggest lever:

### Ask for a raise

Prepare a short case: your results, added responsibilities and what similar roles pay. Ask for a specific number. If the answer is no, ask what you'd need to do to earn it and when you can revisit.

### Take overtime or extra shifts

If available, overtime can add up quickly. Make sure it doesn't burn you out.

### Change jobs

Switching employers is often one of the biggest income jumps. Look at postings for your role to see the range, and consider certifications that can raise your pay.

### Use employer benefits

Some employers offer tuition help, student loan assistance or bonuses for referrals or certifications.

## Side income ideas

### Use skills you already have

- **Freelancing:** writing, design, bookkeeping, web development, video editing
- **Tutoring** in school subjects, test prep, music or languages
- **Consulting** in your professional field, if your employer allows it
- **Virtual assistance** and admin work

### Flexible gig work

- **Food or package delivery**
- **Rideshare driving**
- **Grocery shopping and errands**
- **Task apps** for assembly, moving and odd jobs

Track mileage and costs. Gig work can add wear to your car, and expenses reduce your real hourly earnings.

### Local services

- **Pet sitting and dog walking**
- **Babysitting**
- **House cleaning**
- **Lawn care, snow removal and handyman work**
- **Moving help**

### Sell what you don't need

- Clothing, electronics, furniture, tools and sports equipment
- A second car, if your household can manage without it

Selling items is one-time income, but it can jump-start a [debt snowball](debt-snowball-method.html) or build a starter emergency fund.

### Content and creative work

Writing, videos, photography or handmade goods can grow over time but usually take longer to earn meaningful money. Treat them as long-term projects, not quick fixes. I share my own journey on [my YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@DanielEMotter).

## Avoid side hustle traps

- **Programs that require you to pay upfront** to "unlock" income, such as some multilevel marketing setups
- **Promises of passive income** with little work
- **Startup costs** you'd put on a credit card
- **Work that conflicts** with your employment agreement
- **Burnout:** set a schedule and an end date for intense seasons

## Taxes on side income

Income from side work is generally **taxable**, whether or not you get a tax form. If you're self-employed, you may owe **self-employment tax** in addition to income tax, and you may need to make **estimated quarterly payments**.

Practical tips:

- **Set aside a percentage** of each payment for taxes in a separate savings account. Many people use 25% to 30% as a starting point, but your situation may differ.
- **Track expenses**, since some business costs may be deductible
- **Check IRS guidance** or ask a tax professional

A surprise tax bill can create new debt, so plan for it.

## Make the extra income count

1. **Open a separate account** for side income so it doesn't mix with spending money
2. **Set aside taxes** first
3. **Send the rest to your target debt** on a regular schedule, such as weekly
4. **Don't raise your lifestyle** to match the new income until your debt goal is met
5. **Track progress** so you can see the impact

## How to choose the right side income

Ask:

- **How much can I realistically earn per hour after costs?**
- **How soon can I start?**
- **How many hours can I give without hurting my job, health or family?**
- **Does it build a skill** that could raise my main income later?

The best option is usually the one that pays well, starts quickly and fits your life.

## A sample plan

| Source | Monthly amount |
| --- | --- |
| Two overtime shifts | $250 |
| Weekend pet sitting | $150 |
| Selling unused items (first two months) | $300 one-time |

Remember to set aside part of the pet-sitting income for taxes. Combine extra income with cuts from your budget for the biggest impact. See [cut expenses to pay off debt](cut-expenses-to-pay-off-debt.html).
