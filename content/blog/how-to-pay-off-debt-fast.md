## Why the size of your payment matters so much

Interest is charged on whatever balance remains, so every extra dollar you pay today stops interest from piling up for months. That's why small increases in your payment make a dramatic difference.

Take **$7,500 of credit card debt at 24% APR**:

| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $250 | About 47 months | About $4,068 |
| $500 | About 19 months | About $1,506 |

Doubling the payment cuts the timeline by **more than half** and saves about **$2,560** in interest. (Estimates assume a fixed rate, monthly interest and no new charges.)

## 12 moves to pay off debt faster

### 1. Stop adding new debt

Remove saved cards from apps, skip "pay later" at checkout and pause new financing. You can't outpay a balance that keeps growing.

### 2. Save a small cushion first

A starter emergency fund keeps surprises from landing on a card and erasing your progress. See [how much emergency fund you need](how-much-emergency-fund.html).

### 3. Write a zero-based budget

Give every dollar a job before the month begins, including a specific dollar amount for extra debt payments. Our [zero-based budget guide](zero-based-budget.html) walks through it.

### 4. Pick one target and focus

Spreading extra money across every debt feels fair but slows you down. Choose the [snowball or avalanche](debt-snowball-vs-avalanche.html) and focus on one debt.

### 5. Cut the big three expenses

Housing, transportation and food usually offer the biggest savings. A cheaper car, a roommate or a grocery plan can free up hundreds a month. More ideas in [cutting expenses to pay off debt](cut-expenses-to-pay-off-debt.html).

### 6. Raise your income, even temporarily

Extra shifts, freelancing, selling things you don't use or a seasonal job can speed up payoff dramatically. Send all of it to debt so it doesn't get absorbed by everyday spending. See [side income ideas](side-income-to-pay-off-debt.html).

### 7. Lower your interest rates

Call each card issuer and ask for a lower APR, especially if you've paid on time. See [how to negotiate a lower interest rate](negotiate-lower-interest-rate.html). With good credit, a [0% balance transfer](balance-transfer-cards.html) or a lower-rate [personal loan](personal-loan-to-pay-off-credit-cards.html) can also help, as long as you stop adding new charges.

### 8. Use every windfall

Tax refunds, bonuses, rebates and cash gifts are perfect debt accelerators. A $1,500 refund applied to a 24% card stops roughly $30 of interest every month that balance would have lingered.

### 9. Pay more than once a month

Making a payment each payday instead of once a month lowers your average daily balance on credit cards, which can slightly reduce interest. It also lines up with your cash flow. On loans, confirm that extra money goes to **principal**. See [how extra payments work](extra-payments-on-loans.html).

### 10. Sell what you're still paying for

If a car payment or a pricey gadget is slowing you down, selling it and buying something cheaper with cash can wipe out a debt in one move. If you owe more than it's worth, see [what to do with an upside-down car loan](upside-down-car-loan.html).

### 11. Automate everything

Set minimums on autopay and schedule your extra payment for the day after payday. Automation removes decisions, and fewer decisions means fewer chances to slip.

### 12. Track progress where you'll see it

Seeing balances fall keeps you going. Use the free [debt tracker](../debt-tracker.html) or a simple chart on the fridge.

## Traps that slow you down

- **Closing your emergency fund to pay debt.** Then the next surprise goes right back on a card.
- **Debt settlement promises that sound too good.** They can damage your credit and come with fees. Read [debt settlement vs. bankruptcy](debt-settlement-vs-bankruptcy.html) first.
- **Payday loans or cash advances** to make payments. See [why payday loans are so costly](payday-loans-why-to-avoid.html).
- **Consolidating without changing habits.** A new loan can free up cards that fill right back up.

## Build a "debt sprint" month

If motivation is low, try a 30-day sprint. For one month, cut every optional expense you can live without: restaurants, subscriptions, extra shopping. Sell three things you don't use and pick up any extra work available. Then send every dollar of the difference to your target debt. A sprint shows you what's possible. Many people find they can sustain more of it than they expected, and the extra payment becomes the new normal.

## A realistic timeline

For many people, a focused plan clears consumer debt within a few years. The exact time depends on your balances, rates and the size of your monthly gap. Use the [payment calculator](../calculate-your-payment.html) to find the monthly amount you need to hit a specific debt-free date, then work backward to find that money in your budget.

Q: What is the fastest way to pay off $10,000 in debt?
A: Lower the interest rate if you can, stop new charges, and pay as much as possible each month toward one debt at a time. At 22% APR, paying $500 a month clears $10,000 in about 26 months with roughly $2,570 of interest. Raising the payment shortens it further.
Q: Is it better to pay off debt in a lump sum or monthly?
A: If you have a windfall and still keep a small emergency fund, paying a lump sum on high-interest debt stops interest immediately and is usually best.
Q: Does paying twice a month help?
A: On credit cards, more frequent payments can slightly reduce interest by lowering your average daily balance. The bigger benefit usually comes from paying more in total.
Q: Should I use savings to pay off debt?
A: Keep a small emergency fund. Using extra savings beyond that to pay off high-interest debt usually saves money, but don't leave yourself with nothing for emergencies.
