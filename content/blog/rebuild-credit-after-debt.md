## Step 1: Know where you stand

Get your free reports from all three bureaus at [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/). List:

- Every negative item: late payments, collections, charge-offs, judgments in some cases, bankruptcy
- The date of first delinquency for each one, which sets when it falls off
- Any errors

Dispute anything inaccurate. See [how to check your credit report and dispute errors](check-credit-report-and-dispute-errors.html).

## Step 2: Stop new damage

Payment history is the biggest part of FICO scores, at about 35% for the general population according to [myFICO](https://www.myfico.com/credit-education/whats-in-your-credit-score). From now on:

- **Set up autopay** for at least the minimum on every account
- **Get current** on any past-due accounts if you can. Call creditors and ask about hardship programs.
- **Build a small emergency fund**, even $500 to $1,000, so a surprise doesn't cause a new missed payment. See [how much emergency fund](how-much-emergency-fund.html).

## Step 3: Deal with collections and charge-offs strategically

Paying a collection doesn't automatically remove it, but:

- Some newer scoring models, like **FICO 9 and 10** and **VantageScore 3.0 and 4.0**, treat paid collections differently, which can help
- A paid status may look better to lenders reviewing your report manually
- It ends collection activity and the risk of a lawsuit

Before paying, **validate the debt**, especially if it's old or you don't recognize it. Know your rights and the statute of limitations in your state, since paying or acknowledging some old debts can restart legal timelines. See [debt collectors: your rights](debt-collectors-your-rights.html).

Medical collections have special rules. See [medical debt: what to do](medical-debt-what-to-do.html).

## Step 4: Add positive credit history

If you have few active accounts in good standing, add one or two carefully.

### Secured credit card

You put down a deposit, often a few hundred dollars, that usually becomes your credit limit. Use it for a small recurring bill and **pay in full every month**. Many secured cards later graduate to unsecured cards and refund the deposit. Check that the card reports to all three bureaus and has low or no fees.

### Credit-builder loan

Offered by some credit unions and community banks. The loan amount is held in a savings account while you make payments, and you get the money at the end. It builds payment history and savings at the same time.

### Becoming an authorized user

A family member with a long, on-time history and low utilization can add you to their card. Their account history may appear on your report. It works only if they manage the card well, and you should agree on whether you'll use the card at all.

### Rent and utility reporting

Some services report rent or utility payments to credit bureaus. Not all scoring models use that data, and some services charge fees. Look into the costs before signing up.

## Step 5: Keep utilization low

Keep card balances well below their limits, especially on a new secured card with a small limit. A $300 limit with a $250 balance is over 80% utilization. Pay before the statement closes. See [credit utilization explained](credit-utilization-explained.html).

## Step 6: Apply sparingly

Each application can mean a hard inquiry. Space out applications and use prequalification tools with soft checks when available.

## Realistic timelines

| Item | Typical reporting time |
| --- | --- |
| Late payments | Up to 7 years |
| Collections and charge-offs | Up to 7 years from the original delinquency |
| Chapter 13 bankruptcy | Up to 7 years |
| Chapter 7 bankruptcy | Up to 10 years |
| Hard inquiries | About 2 years, with impact usually fading sooner |

**Impact fades before the item disappears.** A two-year-old late payment generally hurts less than a recent one, especially when it's surrounded by positive history.

## Rebuilding after bankruptcy

Bankruptcy is serious, but it can be a fresh start. Many people begin rebuilding with a secured card within months of discharge. Confirm that discharged debts show a **$0 balance** and **"included in bankruptcy"** on your reports. Dispute any that don't. See [debt settlement vs. bankruptcy](debt-settlement-vs-bankruptcy.html).

## Rebuilding after debt settlement

Settled accounts often show as **"settled for less than the full balance"** and usually stay on your report for up to seven years from the original delinquency. Focus on new positive history. Also check whether forgiven debt has tax consequences, since canceled debt can sometimes be taxable. Ask a tax professional.

## Avoid these traps

- **Credit repair companies** that charge upfront or promise to remove accurate items. Federal law restricts upfront fees and they can't legally remove accurate, timely information.
- **"New credit identity"** schemes using an EIN or CPN. These can be illegal.
- **High-cost loans** marketed to people with bad credit, such as payday loans. See [why to avoid payday loans](payday-loans-why-to-avoid.html).
- **Opening many cards at once** to "rebuild faster"

## A 12-month rebuilding plan

1. **Month 1:** pull reports, dispute errors, set up autopay
2. **Months 1 to 3:** build a starter emergency fund; get current on past-due accounts
3. **Month 2 or 3:** open one secured card or credit-builder loan if needed
4. **Every month:** pay on time, keep utilization low, track progress
5. **Month 6:** check reports again and confirm updates
6. **Month 12:** review progress; consider asking for a limit increase or graduating a secured card

@faq
Q: How long does it take to rebuild credit?
A: It depends on what happened. Many people see progress within months of on-time payments and lower balances. Serious negative items like collections and bankruptcy lose impact over time but can be reported for seven to ten years.
Q: Is a secured credit card a good way to rebuild credit?
A: It can be, if it has low fees, reports to all three bureaus, and you keep the balance low and pay in full every month.
Q: Should I pay off old collections to rebuild credit?
A: It can help with some newer scoring models and stops collection activity. Validate the debt first and understand your state's statute of limitations before paying old debts.
Q: Can a credit repair company fix my credit faster?
A: Not legitimately. You can dispute errors yourself for free, and no one can legally remove accurate, timely negative information.
