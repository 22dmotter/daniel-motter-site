## How car loan interest works

Most auto loans are **simple interest** loans. Interest is calculated on the outstanding principal, often daily, so paying principal down sooner reduces future interest. A few loans, especially in subprime lending, use **precomputed interest**. With those, paying early saves less. Check your contract or ask the lender.

## Example: how much paying early saves

**Loan:** $15,000 at **9% APR** for **48 months**. The payment is about **$373.28**, and total interest is about **$2,917**.

| Monthly payment | Months to payoff | Total interest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $373.28 | 48 | About $2,917 |
| $523.28 (+$150) | About 33 | About $1,952 |

The extra $150 a month saves about **$965** and **15 months** of payments.

## When paying off your car early makes sense

- Your **rate is high**, for example above what a high-yield savings account pays
- You have **no higher-interest debt**, especially credit cards
- You have an **emergency fund** of at least a few months of expenses. See [how much emergency fund](how-much-emergency-fund.html).
- You're **contributing enough to retirement** to get any employer match
- You want to **free up cash flow** for other goals, or you plan to sell the car
- Your loan has **no prepayment penalty**

## When it might not make sense

- Your rate is **very low**, such as a promotional 0% to 3% rate. Extra cash may be better saved or used on higher-rate debt.
- You'd **drain your savings** to do it. A car repair or job loss with no cushion can push you onto credit cards.
- You have **credit card or other high-rate debt**. Pay that first with the [avalanche method](debt-avalanche-method.html).
- Your loan uses **precomputed interest** or has a **prepayment penalty** that wipes out the savings.

## Check for prepayment penalties

Read your retail installment contract or loan agreement for terms like **"prepayment penalty"** or **"precomputed interest."** Penalty rules vary by state and lender. If you can't tell, call the lender and ask:

1. "Is there any fee for paying the loan off early?"
2. "Is interest simple interest or precomputed?"
3. "How do I make a principal-only payment?"

## Car loans vs. other debts

A car loan is secured by the car, so falling behind can lead to repossession. That's a reason to always make the required payment on time. But because auto loan rates are often lower than credit card rates, extra money usually goes further on cards first.

## How to pay off a car loan faster

### Make principal-only payments

When you pay extra, tell the lender to apply it to **principal**, not to next month's payment. Many lenders have a "principal only" option online. Verify it on your next statement.

### Round up or pay biweekly

Rounding $373.28 up to $400 adds about $27 a month. Biweekly half-payments can add about one extra payment a year, if your lender applies them correctly.

### Use windfalls

Tax refunds and bonuses can knock months off the loan.

### Refinance if your rate is high

If your credit has improved since you bought the car, refinancing could lower your rate. Avoid extending the term just to lower the payment.

## A quick decision guide

| Your situation | Likely best move |
| --- | --- |
| Credit card debt at 20%+ | Pay the cards first, minimums on the car |
| No emergency fund | Build a starter fund, then pay extra |
| Car loan at 8% or more, other debts handled | Paying extra is a strong choice |
| Car loan at 0% to 3% | Make regular payments and build savings |
| Planning to sell soon | Paying down principal helps avoid negative equity |

There's no single right answer. A guaranteed savings equal to your interest rate is valuable, but so is having cash on hand. Many people split the difference, such as sending half of their extra money to the car and half to savings until the emergency fund is full.

## What about your credit score?

Paying off an installment loan early can sometimes cause a **small, temporary dip** in your score, since an active account in good standing closes and your credit mix changes. That's usually minor compared to the interest savings and freedom of being debt-free. See [does paying off debt raise your credit score](does-paying-off-debt-raise-credit-score.html).

## After the car is paid off

The best move after your final payment: **keep "paying" yourself.** Send the old payment amount to savings or your next debt. In 12 months, a $373 payment redirected to savings becomes almost $4,500 for your next car, repairs or emergencies, which helps you avoid another loan. Also contact the lender to confirm the **lien release** and get your title.

Q: Does paying off a car loan early hurt your credit?
A: It can cause a small, temporary dip because an active installment account closes. The long-term effect is usually minor, and you save interest.
Q: Is there a penalty for paying off a car loan early?
A: Many auto loans have no prepayment penalty, but some do, and some use precomputed interest that reduces savings. Check your contract or ask your lender.
Q: Is it better to pay off a car loan or credit card first?
A: Usually the credit card, because card interest rates are typically much higher than auto loan rates.
Q: How do I make extra payments go to principal on my car loan?
A: Use the lender's principal-only payment option or include written instructions, then check your statement to confirm the extra amount reduced the principal.
