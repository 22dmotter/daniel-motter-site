Budgeting & saving

How to cut expenses to pay off debt faster: 25 practical ideas

By Daniel Motter · Nov 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: The fastest way to free up money for debt is to cut big fixed costs first, such as housing, transportation, insurance and subscriptions, because one change keeps saving every month. Then trim variable spending like food and shopping. Most importantly, send each dollar you save directly to your target debt the same day, or it tends to disappear. Cutting $300 a month and sending it to a $10,000 credit card at 22% can shave years off payoff.

Start with a spending audit

Before cutting, look at what you actually spend. Pull 60 to 90 days of bank and card statements and sort every charge into categories. Highlight:

  • Recurring charges you'd forgotten about
  • Categories much higher than you expected, often food
  • Fees: overdraft, late, ATM, foreign transaction

This step alone often finds money. Many banking apps can sort transactions into categories automatically, which makes the audit faster.

What $300 a month can do

Example: $10,000 of credit card debt at 22% APR. Paying $300 a month, it takes about 52 months and costs about $5,596 of interest. Raising the payment to $600 a month cuts that to about 21 months and about $2,043 of interest, saving roughly $3,550.

Big fixed costs

Housing

  1. Get a roommate or rent a room
  2. Move somewhere cheaper at the end of your lease
  3. Negotiate rent at renewal, especially if you've been a reliable tenant
  4. Refinance a mortgage if rates and closing costs make sense

Transportation

  1. Sell a car you can't afford and buy a cheaper, reliable one
  2. Go down to one car if your household can manage
  3. Refinance a high-rate auto loan. See pay off car loan early.
  4. Use transit, biking or carpooling where practical

Insurance

  1. Shop auto and home or renters insurance every year or two
  2. Raise deductibles once your emergency fund can cover them
  3. Bundle policies if it lowers total cost

Phone, internet and subscriptions

  1. Switch to a lower-cost phone plan
  2. Call your internet provider and ask about current promotions or cheaper plans
  3. Cancel unused subscriptions, and rotate streaming services instead of keeping them all
  4. Check app store subscriptions, which are easy to forget

Everyday spending

Food

  1. Plan meals for the week and shop with a list
  2. Cook in batches and pack lunches
  3. Cut back on delivery, which often includes fees and higher menu prices
  4. Buy store brands for staples

Shopping

  1. Use a 48-hour rule for nonessential purchases
  2. Unsubscribe from store emails and remove saved cards from shopping sites
  3. Buy used for furniture, tools and kids' gear

Fees and interest

  1. Ask for late fees to be waived and set up autopay to avoid more
  2. Opt out of overdraft coverage or set balance alerts
  3. Call card issuers for a lower rate. See negotiate a lower interest rate.

Make the savings count

Cutting expenses only speeds up debt payoff if the money actually goes to debt. The best approach:

  1. Decide the amount you're freeing up, such as $150 from a phone and insurance change
  2. Increase your automatic debt payment by that amount right away
  3. Send one-time savings, like a refund or sold item, to debt the same day
  4. Track it, so you can see the impact

This is how small changes turn into real progress with the debt snowball or avalanche.

Don't cut so deep you quit

Extreme budgets often fail. Keep:

  • A small amount of fun money
  • Essentials for health and work
  • Some giving, if that's important to you
  • A starter emergency fund, so surprises don't create new debt

A plan you can follow for two years beats a perfect plan you abandon in two months.

Cutting expenses with a partner or family

Talk about goals before cuts. Agree on what matters most, pick shared changes, and let each person keep a little personal money. Celebrate milestones together, like each debt paid off.

Pair cutting with earning

There's a limit to how much you can cut, but there's more room to grow income. Combining both is the fastest route. See side income to pay off debt.

Frequently asked questions

What expenses should I cut first to pay off debt?

Start with big fixed costs like housing, transportation, insurance and subscriptions, since one change saves money every month. Then trim food and shopping.

How much can cutting expenses speed up debt payoff?

It depends on your debt and rates. For example, raising payments on $10,000 at 22% from $300 to $600 a month cuts payoff from about 52 months to about 21 and saves about $3,550 in interest.

Should I cut all fun spending while paying off debt?

Usually not. A small amount of fun money makes a budget easier to stick with over the long run.

How do I make sure the money I save goes to debt?

Increase your automatic debt payment by the amount you've freed up, and send one-time savings to your debt the same day.
