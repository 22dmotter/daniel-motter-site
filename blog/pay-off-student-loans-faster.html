Loans: student, car, personal & home

How to pay off student loans faster: 9 strategies that work

By Daniel Motter · Nov 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: To pay off student loans faster, pay more than the minimum and make sure the extra goes to principal on your highest-rate loan, then use the avalanche or snowball method across loans. Add windfalls, use autopay discounts, and consider refinancing private loans at a lower rate. For example, adding $100 a month to a $30,000 loan at 6% on a 10-year plan cuts payoff from 120 to about 86 months and saves about $3,046 in interest. Don't rush if you're pursuing PSLF or IDR forgiveness.

First: should you pay them off faster?

Paying faster saves interest, but it isn't always the best move:

  • Pursuing PSLF or IDR forgiveness? Extra payments may just reduce the amount that would be forgiven. See student loan forgiveness basics.
  • No emergency fund? Build a starter cushion first. See pay off debt or save first.
  • Higher-rate debt elsewhere? Credit cards at 20%+ usually come first.
  • Missing out on an employer retirement match? That match is often worth getting first. See pay off debt or invest.

If none of these apply, paying faster is a solid, guaranteed return equal to your interest rate.

9 strategies to pay off student loans faster

1. Target extra money at the highest-rate loan

You probably have several loans with different rates. Pay minimums on all of them, and send extra to the highest rate. That's the debt avalanche. If motivation matters more, target the smallest balance first with the snowball.

2. Make sure extra goes to principal

Servicers may apply extra payments to future installments or spread them across loans. Tell your servicer, in writing or in your online account settings, to apply extra amounts to principal on a specific loan and not to advance your due date. Check your statement afterward.

3. Use the autopay discount

Many federal servicers and private lenders offer a small interest rate reduction, commonly 0.25%, for automatic payments. It's free money.

4. Pay biweekly or round up

Paying half the payment every two weeks results in 26 half-payments, or 13 full payments a year. Rounding $333.06 up to $350 adds about $17 a month without much pain. Confirm your servicer applies early or partial payments correctly.

5. Put windfalls to work

Tax refunds, bonuses, raises and gifts can make big dents. Consider splitting them, such as 75% to loans and 25% to savings or fun, to stay motivated.

6. Refinance private loans at a lower rate

If you have high-rate private loans, refinancing can cut interest. Be careful with federal loans, since refinancing them ends federal protections. See should I refinance my student loans.

7. Ask about employer repayment help

Some employers offer student loan repayment assistance as a benefit. Check your benefits guide or ask HR.

8. Increase income

A side gig, overtime or a raise can speed things up dramatically. See side income to pay off debt.

9. Cut expenses deliberately

Redirect savings from subscriptions, insurance shopping or housing changes directly to loans. See cut expenses to pay off debt.

Stay consistent

Small, steady extra payments usually beat occasional big ones. Automating even $25 or $50 extra each month builds momentum, and you can raise it whenever your income grows.

Pay interest while in school or grace period

Unsubsidized federal and most private loans accrue interest while you're in school and during grace periods. That interest may capitalize, or be added to principal, at certain points. Paying the interest as it accrues, even small amounts, can keep your balance from growing.

Make it visible

Progress is motivating. Track every payment and watch the balance fall. Use our debt payoff calculator to see your payoff date under different extra-payment amounts, and the debt tracker to log progress privately in your browser.

A simple monthly routine

  1. On payday, pay every loan's required amount first.
  2. Move your chosen extra amount to the target loan the same day, before it gets spent.
  3. Log the new balance in your tracker.
  4. Once a quarter, check that extra payments went to principal and that your autopay discount is still active.
  5. When a loan is paid off, roll its full payment into the next target loan.

Common mistakes

  • Paying extra without telling the servicer how to apply it
  • Rushing payoff while carrying high-interest credit card debt
  • Skipping an employer retirement match to pay a low-rate loan
  • Refinancing federal loans without considering lost protections
  • Draining all savings to make a lump-sum payment

Frequently asked questions

Is it better to pay off student loans early or invest?

It depends on your interest rate, risk tolerance and whether you have an employer match. Paying off a loan earns a guaranteed return equal to its rate. Many people do both: get the match, then pay extra on higher-rate loans.

Are there penalties for paying off student loans early?

Federal student loans have no prepayment penalty. Most private student loans don't either, but check your loan agreement.

How do I make sure extra payments go to principal?

Tell your servicer to apply extra amounts to principal on a specific loan and not to advance your due date. Use your online account settings or written instructions, and check your statement afterward.

Which student loan should I pay off first?

The avalanche method targets the highest interest rate first and saves the most. The snowball method targets the smallest balance first for quick wins. Both work if you stay consistent.
