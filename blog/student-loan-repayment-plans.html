Loans: student, car, personal & home

Student loan repayment plans in 2026: RAP, standard and IBR explained

By Daniel Motter · Nov 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: For federal Direct Loans, the main options in 2026 are fixed plans (Standard and the new Tiered Standard plan) and income-driven plans, mainly the new Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) and Income-Based Repayment (IBR). The SAVE plan ended in March 2026, and borrowers whose loans were all disbursed on or after July 1, 2026 can use only RAP or Tiered Standard. RAP payments are 1% to 10% of adjusted gross income, minus $50 per dependent, with a $10 minimum. Confirm your options on studentaid.gov.

Things changed a lot in 2026

Federal student loan repayment was overhauled by a 2025 law. Key changes, according to Federal Student Aid (studentaid.gov):

  • The SAVE Plan ended after a court order in March 2026. SAVE borrowers must choose another plan, or they'll be placed in a fixed-payment plan.
  • The new Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP) became available on July 1, 2026.
  • A new Tiered Standard Plan applies to new loans.
  • If you take out a new loan or consolidate on or after July 1, 2026, your Direct Loans must be repaid under RAP or the Tiered Standard Plan.
  • Some older plans, including PAYE and ICR, are being phased out. The U.S. Department of Education says certain borrowers with loans made before July 1, 2026 have until July 1, 2028 to choose among RAP, Tiered Standard or IBR.

Rules may continue to change through regulations and court decisions, so treat this as an overview and confirm details with your servicer or on studentaid.gov.

Fixed-payment plans

Standard Repayment Plan

For borrowers with older loans, the classic standard plan has fixed payments over up to 10 years (longer for some consolidation loans). It usually costs the least total interest of any plan.

Example: $30,000 at 6% over 10 years is about $333.06 a month, with about $9,967 of total interest.

Tiered Standard Plan

For loans made on or after July 1, 2026, the Tiered Standard Plan offers fixed payments with a repayment period that depends on how much you owe. Check the current terms on studentaid.gov or with your servicer.

Income-driven repayment (IDR) plans

Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)

According to Federal Student Aid:

  • Payments are 1% to 10% of your adjusted gross income (AGI), divided by 12, depending on income
  • Payments are reduced by $50 per month for each dependent you claim
  • The minimum payment is $10 a month
  • Any remaining balance may be forgiven after 30 years of qualifying payments, or sooner through PSLF if you qualify
  • Includes an interest subsidy and principal match for qualifying borrowers, designed so balances don't grow when payments are low
  • Parent PLUS loans and consolidation loans that repaid Parent PLUS loans are not eligible
  • FFEL Program loans don't qualify unless consolidated into Direct Loans, subject to the rules above

Income-Based Repayment (IBR)

Available for loans disbursed before July 1, 2026. Payments are 15% of discretionary income (10% for "new borrowers" as of July 1, 2014), capped at the 10-year standard payment amount, with forgiveness after 25 years (20 for new borrowers).

Plans being phased out

PAYE and ICR are ending, no later than July 1, 2028 according to studentaid.gov. If you're enrolled, watch for servicer notices about your deadline to switch.

How to choose a plan

If your priority is...Consider...
Lowest total interestStandard (or Tiered Standard) and extra payments
Lowest monthly paymentAn income-driven plan like RAP or IBR
Public Service Loan ForgivenessA PSLF-qualifying plan, usually an IDR plan
Flexibility after income dropsAn IDR plan, recertified each year

Use the Loan Simulator on studentaid.gov to compare payments under each plan using your real loans.

A few important cautions

  • Lower payments usually mean more interest and a longer time in debt, unless subsidies or forgiveness offset it.
  • Recertify income annually for IDR plans, or your payment may jump.
  • Don't miss the SAVE transition deadline. If you were on SAVE, choose a new plan before your servicer's deadline.
  • Consolidating after July 1, 2026 limits your plan choices. Think carefully before consolidating.
  • Private student loans don't qualify for federal plans. Contact your lender about their hardship options.

If you want to pay off faster

The math rewards aggressive payment if you're not pursuing forgiveness. Adding $100 a month to the $30,000, 6%, 10-year example cuts payoff to about 86 months and saves about $3,046. See how to pay off student loans faster.

If you're on an IDR plan aiming for forgiveness, or working toward PSLF, extra payments may not help you. See student loan forgiveness basics.

Free official help

You never need to pay for help choosing a federal repayment plan. Use studentaid.gov or call your loan servicer. Be wary of companies charging fees for something you can do for free.

Frequently asked questions

What happened to the SAVE plan?

According to Federal Student Aid, a court order ended the SAVE Plan in March 2026. Enrolled borrowers must choose another plan by their servicer's deadline, or they'll be placed in a fixed-payment plan.

What is the Repayment Assistance Plan (RAP)?

RAP is an income-driven plan that started July 1, 2026. Payments are 1% to 10% of adjusted gross income, minus $50 per dependent, with a $10 minimum, and any remaining balance may be forgiven after 30 years.

Can Parent PLUS loans use RAP?

No. According to Federal Student Aid, Parent PLUS loans and consolidation loans that repaid them are not eligible for RAP.

Which student loan repayment plan is best?

It depends on your income, balance, career and goals. The standard plan usually costs the least overall. Income-driven plans lower payments and may lead to forgiveness. Compare options with the Loan Simulator on studentaid.gov.
