Loans: student, car, personal & home

Should I use home equity to pay off debt? HELOCs, loans and risks

By Daniel Motter · Nov 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: Using home equity to pay off high-interest debt can lower your interest rate, but it turns unsecured debt like credit cards into debt secured by your house. If you can't repay, you could lose your home. It can make sense only if you have stable income, a firm payoff plan with a short term, and you've fixed the habits that created the debt. Otherwise, a personal loan, balance transfer or nonprofit debt management plan is usually safer.

Three ways to tap home equity

Home equity loan

A lump sum with a fixed rate and fixed payments, repaid over a set term. It's often called a second mortgage.

Home equity line of credit (HELOC)

A revolving line you can draw from, usually with a variable rate. There's often a draw period, sometimes with interest-only payments, followed by a repayment period when payments can rise sharply.

Cash-out refinance

You replace your mortgage with a larger new mortgage and take the difference in cash. This resets your mortgage rate and term, which can be costly if your current rate is lower than today's rates.

How much equity can you use?

Equity is your home's value minus what you owe. Lenders typically limit total borrowing on the home to a percentage of its value, often called the combined loan-to-value ratio. For example, if your home is worth $350,000, you owe $230,000, and a lender allows 80% combined loan-to-value, the most you could borrow is about $50,000 ($280,000 minus $230,000). Limits vary by lender.

Two lessons from this table:

  1. A lower rate with a long term saves surprisingly little. The 10-year option has a much lower payment, but only saves about $3,800 because you pay interest for twice as long.
  2. A lower rate with a short term saves a lot: about $10,200 compared with the cards.

The examples ignore closing costs and fees, which can be significant. Variable-rate HELOC payments can also change.

The biggest risk: your home

Credit card debt is unsecured. If you can't pay, it hurts your credit and can lead to collections or lawsuits, but the card issuer can't simply take your house. When you pay cards off with home equity, that same debt is now secured by your home. Missed payments can lead to foreclosure.

This risk is highest if:

  • Your income is unstable
  • You don't have an emergency fund
  • You might run the cards back up, leaving you with the home equity loan and new card debt
  • Home values fall, leaving you owing more than the home is worth

Other costs and catches

  • Closing costs and appraisal fees
  • Variable rates on most HELOCs
  • Payment shock when a HELOC's draw period ends
  • Longer debt timelines if you stretch the term
  • Tax rules: interest on home equity debt used for non-home purposes generally isn't deductible under current federal rules. Check with a tax professional.

Questions to ask a lender

  • Is the rate fixed or variable, and how often can it change?
  • What are the total closing costs and fees?
  • Is there a minimum draw or an early closure fee?
  • When does the draw period end, and what will the payment be after that?
  • Can you pay the creditors directly from the loan?

When it can make sense

  • You have substantial equity, steady income and an emergency fund
  • You've stopped using credit cards and have a written budget
  • You choose a fixed rate and short term and pay it like a must-pay bill
  • You've compared it with unsecured options and the savings are clearly worth the added risk

If you do use home equity

  1. Get quotes from several lenders, including a credit union.
  2. Prefer a fixed-rate home equity loan with a short term for debt payoff.
  3. Pay off the cards immediately and confirm $0 balances.
  4. Lock or put away the cards.
  5. Automate payments and add extra when possible. See extra payments on loans.
  6. Keep building your emergency fund so surprises don't go on a card.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a good idea to use a HELOC to pay off credit cards?

It can lower your rate, but it puts your home at risk if you can't pay, and most HELOCs have variable rates. It only makes sense with stable income, a short payoff plan and no new card debt.

What is the difference between a HELOC and a home equity loan?

A home equity loan is a lump sum with a fixed rate and payment. A HELOC is a revolving line of credit, usually with a variable rate and a draw period followed by a repayment period.

Can I lose my house if I don't pay a home equity loan?

Yes. Home equity loans and HELOCs are secured by your home, so serious default can lead to foreclosure.

Is a cash-out refinance better than a HELOC for paying off debt?

It depends on your current mortgage rate, today's rates and closing costs. A cash-out refinance replaces your entire mortgage, so it can be costly if your current rate is low.
